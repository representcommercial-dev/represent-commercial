How this is calculated

Property value

The future value of the premises is grown from the purchase price at the annual capital growth rate you select.

Financing

Repayments and loan balances follow the loan structure, rate and term you enter. On a principal and interest loan the balance amortises each year and the repayment splits into interest and principal. On an interest only loan the balance stays where it started, so equity comes from growth alone.

Leasing

Rent starts at the year-one figure you enter and escalates at the annual review. Tenant outgoings are added on top and escalate at the ownership cost growth rate.

Ownership costs

Rates, land tax, insurance, upkeep and capital reserve start at the figure you enter and increase each year at the ownership cost growth rate.

Alternative capital

The lease scenario starts with the cash the purchase would have absorbed — deposit plus acquisition costs, less any initial lease costs — and compounds it at the alternative return. Each year the difference between the cost of owning (repayments plus ownership costs) and the cost of leasing (rent plus outgoings) is added to or drawn from that capital, so both strategies assume the same total spend on premises. That is what makes the two sides comparable: every dollar of acquisition cost, interest and ownership cost sits on the lease side of the ledger as capital the tenant still holds.

Net advantage

At each year the modelled ownership position is compared against the modelled lease position. Where a sale is assumed at the end of the period, disposal costs are deducted from the ownership position; where it is not, property equity is shown gross and net sale proceeds appear separately for reference.

What is excluded

Taxation, depreciation and GST effects are excluded from the model because outcomes depend on ownership structure, transaction structure and individual circumstances. Vacancy, incentives, market rent reviews and refinancing are also outside the model.
