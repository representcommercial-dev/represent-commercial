Managing my property

For owners of commercial property who want it managed well, or who need senior oversight above their existing manager.

Getting started

What does a commercial property manager actually do?

Rent invoicing and collection, arrears, outgoings budgeting and reconciliation, lease administration and critical-date tracking, statutory compliance, maintenance and contractor coordination, inspections and owner reporting. Beyond the administration, the work that changes the result is rent review positioning, renewal strategy, vacancy planning and expenditure decisions. Owners who need strategic direction above the day-to-day can add commercial asset management.

Do you manage property you did not lease or sell?

Yes. Most engagements begin with a property already tenanted, managed elsewhere, or managed by the owner directly. A common starting point is a Commercial Property Performance Review: a free written report on the property’s current position, three priorities and recommended next steps.

Will I deal with Nick directly?

Yes. Nick O’Toole, Founder and Director, is the person advising you and the person accountable. Your property is not handed to a junior manager after the appointment. Every management tier includes a direct line to the principal. Active adds an annual asset strategy session with Nick, and Portfolio adds quarterly portfolio strategy sessions. Asset management engagements are led by Nick personally.

Where do you manage property?

Property management and leasing are available in Brisbane, for office, retail and industrial property. Asset management is available throughout Queensland and is delivered remotely, with site visits where the project needs them. If you are unsure whether a property falls within our coverage, send the address and we will confirm.

Asset management and fractional oversight

How is asset management different from property management?

A property manager administers the asset: rent, outgoings, compliance, maintenance and reporting. An asset manager decides what the asset should be doing and holds the property manager to it. We sit above your existing manager and set the direction; they keep running the day-to-day. See commercial property management for the day-to-day service we provide in Brisbane.

What is fractional asset management?

It is senior asset management bought by the project rather than by salary. You get the strategic oversight an institutional owner would have in-house: someone setting the plan for the asset, directing the property manager, approving capital works within the authority set out in your written engagement, and running lease events, without employing a full-time asset manager. The services and project types are set out on our commercial asset management page.

Who is fractional asset management suited to?

Syndicators, family offices and private owners holding one or more commercial assets who need senior oversight but cannot justify a full-time asset manager. It usually suits an owner facing a specific decision: a major expiry, option or market review, a capital works program, investors who expect more than a monthly ledger, a regional asset where the local manager is the only source of information, or a choice between holding, improving and selling.

Will an asset manager try to replace my property manager?

No. We oversee your property manager; we do not take over the management. If the arrangement is not working we will tell you plainly and explain why, but the engagement is not a pitch for the management appointment.

Changing managers

Can I change managers mid-lease?

Yes. The tenant’s lease is between you and the tenant, and it continues unchanged when you change managers. The management appointment is a separate agreement between you and your manager, so it can be ended under its own terms, including any notice period it sets.

How do I change property managers?

You do not need to manage the change yourself. Once you appoint us, we prepare the notice to your current manager for you to sign, then coordinate the handover directly with them: lease documents, tenant ledgers, trust balances, bonds and bank guarantees, keys, contractor details and critical dates. We also write to each tenant with the new payment and contact details, so they know where to pay rent from the changeover date.

Income, outgoings and reporting

How are rent collection and arrears handled?

Rent is invoiced, collected and banked as part of every management tier, with arrears monitored and followed up as they arise. Arrears are easier to recover early, before the debt grows and the tenant’s position changes. Where arrears persist, the options depend on the lease and any security held, and we set them out for you to decide. You receive monthly owner statements and live access to the asset through the owner portal.

How are outgoings budgeted, reconciled and recovered?

Outgoings are budgeted before each year and reconciled against actual spend afterwards, with tenants charged or credited for the difference as their leases allow. What can be recovered is set by each lease rather than by a general rule, so a net lease, a gross lease and a retail shop lease can treat the same cost differently. Retail shop leases in Queensland also carry statutory requirements, including an audited annual statement of outgoings. The Active tier reviews whether you are recovering everything the leases permit.

What reporting will I receive?

Every tier includes monthly owner statements and live access to the asset through the owner portal. Active adds an annual asset performance review, capital expenditure reporting and monitoring of income risk and WALE. Portfolio adds consolidated portfolio reporting, benchmarking, cash flow forecasting and syndicate or multi-owner reporting, with the reporting cadence set to your requirements. The aim is reporting that tells you what is coming and what to do about it, not only what happened.

Maintenance and capital works

How are maintenance and capital expenditure decided?

Routine maintenance and contractor coordination are part of every tier. Capital works are a separate decision, made by you or within the spending authority set out in your written management agreement. They should be scoped, quoted and sequenced against the hold strategy and the lease obligations, rather than approved one quote at a time. Active adds planned and preventative maintenance scheduling and minor capital works coordination. Portfolio adds capital expenditure forecasting and lifecycle planning. Where a larger program needs owner-side leadership, it can be run as a commercial asset management project.

Lease events and planning

How are rent reviews, options and lease expiries managed?

Critical dates for every lease are tracked so reviews are actioned on time and option and expiry dates are not missed. Under the Active tier, market and CPI reviews are managed proactively and renewals are negotiated to a strategy agreed with you. Leasing starts before the space is empty: renewal intent is tested early and the rent position is set against evidence ahead of the expiry. The Rent Review Calculator projects rent across a lease term under fixed, CPI or greater-of reviews.

What should I do if a tenant may not renew?

Find out early. A tenant’s intentions, the market rent and the condition of the space all shape whether retention or re-leasing is the better result, and both take time to prepare. Retention may justify an incentive or works; re-leasing needs a campaign brief, property information and an asking rent supported by evidence before the space falls vacant. Our commercial property leasing page explains how a campaign is run.

A free formal written report outlining your property’s current position, three priorities and recommended next steps. After receiving the report, you can discuss the findings with Nick by phone or video call. It is not a valuation, building inspection, engineering report, or legal, tax or financial advice. The right outcome may be to continue the current approach, adjust one part of it or take no immediate action, and any ongoing services are optional. Request a Commercial Property Performance Review.

Leasing my property

For owners with vacant space, or a tenancy approaching expiry, who need a tenant.

Getting started

Can you lease a property you don’t manage?

Yes. Leasing can be a standalone engagement. Where we also manage the property, leasing starts before the space is empty: renewal intent tested early and the rent position set ahead of the expiry rather than after it. See commercial property management for what the ongoing appointment covers.

How should I prepare a property for lease?

Review make-good, services and presentation, then assemble the information a tenant needs to qualify the space before it goes to market: areas, services, clearance, access, parking, permitted use, outgoings and a floor plan. Identify the likely tenant profile and the competing stock. A listing that answers those questions up front gets enquiries from tenants who can actually use the space, rather than questions that delay every inspection.

Where do you lease property?

Pricing and rent

How is the asking rent set?

From comparable leasing evidence, rather than from what the owner needs or what the property achieved three years ago. We track lease and sale transactions in the Brisbane industrial precincts we work in and publish the medians. That evidence sets the asking rent, supports it when a tenant pushes back, and tells you early if the market disagrees with the price. Recent medians for each precinct are on our precinct data pages.

What is the difference between net rent and gross rent?

Under a net lease, the tenant pays rent plus a share of the property’s outgoings, such as rates, insurance and building costs, as the lease specifies. Under a gross lease, the rent includes outgoings and the owner meets them. Many leases sit in between. Tenants compare premises on total occupancy cost, so an asking rent should be presented with the outgoings position stated clearly. The Lease Incentive & Effective Rent Calculator works in net or gross terms.

Do I have to drop the rent?

Not necessarily, but the asking rent needs a defensible basis. Where the price sits above the evidence, the incentive, fitout contribution or term usually has to carry the difference. That should be a deliberate decision with the net effective position understood, not something discovered after six months of no enquiry. The Vacancy vs Rent Reduction Calculator shows the break-even point between holding out and accepting a lower rent, and the Lease Incentive & Effective Rent Calculator converts a face rent and incentive into net effective rent.

How do incentives affect the rent I actually receive?

An incentive reduces what the lease is worth even when the face rent holds. It may take the form of rent-free months, a rent abatement spread across the term, or a contribution to the tenant’s fitout. Comparing offers on net effective rent, after the incentive is spread across the term, shows which one actually pays more. How an incentive is structured also affects cash flow in the early years and the rent evidence the lease creates.

Why is my property still vacant?

Usually one of four things: the asking rent is above what recent evidence supports, the listing does not answer the questions a tenant needs answered, enquiries are not being responded to quickly enough, or inspections are too hard to arrange. Each is fixable, but each needs to be identified rather than assumed. It depends on the precinct, the space, the price and how the property is presented, and no honest answer is a fixed number of weeks. What we can tell you at the outset is what comparable space has been leasing for, and how long comparable campaigns have taken in that precinct. Median lease rates and lease terms from the last 12 months of transactions in the industrial precincts we track are on our precinct data pages.

How are enquiries and inspections handled?

Enquiries come to Nick and are acknowledged promptly, with the information the tenant needs to qualify the space attached rather than promised. Inspections are arranged without unnecessary steps, including outside standard hours where a tenant’s operation requires it, and Nick attends rather than sending a key. You receive feedback on who enquired, what they asked and why they passed, so decisions about price and presentation rest on what the market is saying.

Tenants, terms and documents

How are prospective tenants qualified, and what security should I ask for?

Qualification tests whether the tenant can use the space and pay for it: the proposed use against the permitted use and approvals, the space and services required, and the business’s trading history and financial position. Security is then set against that covenant and against what the owner is contributing in incentive or works. Common forms are a bank guarantee or cash bond and a personal or director guarantee. The amount is negotiated, so it should be considered alongside the rent and incentive rather than after them.

What happens once a tenant agrees terms?

The agreed terms are recorded in a heads of agreement covering rent, term, options, reviews, incentive, outgoings, security, make-good and any conditions. The lease is then prepared by your solicitor, who advises on the legal documents. We negotiate the commercial terms and coordinate documentation through to occupation. For a retail shop lease in Queensland, the owner must give the tenant a disclosure statement and the proposed lease at least 7 days before the lease is entered into, which affects the timetable.

What should I check about permitted use and approvals?

The tenant’s proposed use needs to be lawful for the site under the planning scheme and any existing approvals, and consistent with the permitted use clause in the lease. A use that needs a new approval adds time, cost and possible conditions, so it should be identified before terms are agreed, not after. Where there is doubt, a town planner should confirm the position. Building works for the tenant’s fitout may also need approval.

Who is responsible for fitout, condition and make-good?

Whatever is agreed. The handover condition, any works the owner will complete, the tenant’s fitout and its approval process, and the condition the premises must be returned in at the end of the lease are all negotiated and then recorded in the lease. Know the condition of the space and the make-good position under the outgoing lease before marketing it. Unclear make-good obligations are a common source of dispute at the end of a tenancy.

Can you help before the existing tenant leaves?

Yes. We can discuss the current tenant’s intentions and plan the next steps around the lease position, expected availability and access arrangements. Starting early means the rent position, campaign brief and property information can be ready before the space falls vacant. Where the tenant holds an option, the lease sets how it is exercised and how the rent is reviewed. A Commercial Property Performance Review looks at the lease position alongside income, expenditure and management.

Search and analysis

Do you look at off-market property?

Yes. The search covers on-market, pre-market and off-market opportunities, including approaches to owners not currently selling. You see a shortlist, with the reasons the rest were excluded. Off-market is not automatically better value. It widens the comparison, and every option is still tested against the same evidence and brief.

How do I know if I am paying too much?

By testing the asking price against comparable sale evidence, the passing rent against market rent, and the outgoings recovery against what the lease actually permits. A price can look acceptable on yield and still be too high if the rent sits above market or the recovery position is weaker than assumed. The Net Yield & Property Value Calculator shows the yield at an asking price, or the value the net income supports at a market yield.

What costs should I allow for beyond the purchase price?

Allow for transfer duty, legal and conveyancing costs, due diligence reports such as building, valuation and planning, and finance costs. GST treatment depends on the property and the contract, so confirm it with your accountant before signing. The total changes the yield you are actually buying at, so test returns on the full cost rather than the price. The Acquisition Cost & Yield Calculator adds these costs to the price and tests yield on cost and cash on cash return.

What makes commercial income sustainable?

Rent that the lease and the market both support, from a tenant able to pay it, after the outgoings the owner carries and cannot recover. One-off income, above-market rent and near-term expiries all reduce what the income is worth. The WALE Calculator shows the weighted average lease expiry and when income falls due for renewal across a tenancy schedule.

What should I check in the leases before buying?

The expiry and option dates, the rent review mechanism, which outgoings are recoverable, the security held and whether it can be transferred to you, make-good obligations, permitted use, and any assignment, sublease or special conditions. Compare the passing rent with market rent, and check the tenant’s ability to keep paying it. A fixed increase above the market’s trajectory can make the income look stronger than it will be at the next market review. The Rent Review Calculator projects rent under fixed or CPI reviews.

How do you assess value-add potential?

We test rental reversion, lease quality, vacancy, presentation, capital works and alternative configurations against market evidence. The assessment allows for costs, approvals, time and downside scenarios. Potential uplift is an assumption to test, not a guaranteed outcome. The Rental Reversion & Value Uplift Calculator measures how far a passing rent sits below market and what capturing the gap is worth at a given yield.

Due diligence, contracts and settlement

What risks should be tested before buying?

The risks most often found late are make-good exposure, unapproved works, contamination, non-recoverable outgoings, capital items falling due, and a tenant weaker than the covenant suggests. Each can change the price you should pay or whether you should buy at all, so they need to be tested within the due diligence period rather than discovered after settlement. Our guide on how to avoid buying the wrong commercial property covers each in more detail.

Who carries out specialist due diligence?

Your legal, valuation, building, town planning, finance and tax advisers carry out their own specialist work. Nick coordinates with them where needed and brings their findings into the assessment and negotiation.

Should I sign a conditional or unconditional contract?

A conditional contract gives you time to complete due diligence and obtain finance before you are committed. An unconditional offer removes those protections and may be stronger on price, but only suits a buyer who has already done the work. The conditions, their periods and what happens if one is not satisfied are set by the contract, so settle them with your solicitor before signing. We negotiate the commercial position, including how long the due diligence and finance periods need to be for the property.

What happens between contract and settlement?

The due diligence and finance conditions are worked through and satisfied or resolved, your solicitor manages the legal steps to settlement, and the leases, security and outgoings are adjusted to you as the new owner. It is also the time to settle the ownership plan, including leasing, rent reviews, property improvements and capital expenditure, so it starts at settlement rather than months later.

Who oversees the property after settlement?

If you separately appoint Represent Commercial as asset manager, Nick oversees the agreed ownership strategy and coordinates the agreed leasing, rental reversion, refurbishment planning, consultants, contractors and performance decisions. Works are carried out by appointed contractors. Asset management is available throughout Queensland. For Brisbane assets, property management and leasing can also be separately agreed. Outside Brisbane, Nick works with a local property manager and leasing agent. You can appoint Represent Commercial for the acquisition alone. See commercial asset management.

How are fees agreed for a premises purchase?

Requirements and the building

How do you define what the business needs?

With a written premises brief based on how the business actually operates: usable space and layout, access for staff, customers and deliveries, parking, services, preferred locations, budget and timing. It also records existing lease commitments and any planned relocation. Agreeing the brief before the search prevents the search from defining the requirement for you, and gives every option a consistent test.

What should I check about location, access and the building?

How the site works day to day: vehicle access and turning, loading, parking, clearance height, power supply, services and the layout of usable space. Then whether it can accommodate growth, and what adapting it would cost. Finally, consider how readily the property would lease or sell if the business relocated, grew or changed direction. A building that suits only your operation can be harder to exit.

Should I buy a strata unit or a freehold property?

A strata unit usually costs less to enter, but you share decisions and costs with other owners through the body corporate. By-laws can restrict use, signage, operating hours and alterations, and levies and planned works affect your holding cost. Freehold gives you control of the whole site and its future, at a higher entry cost. For a strata purchase, your solicitor should review the by-laws, body corporate records and levies before you commit.

How do planning, permitted use and fitout affect a purchase?

Your business’s use needs to be lawful on the site, and the building needs to suit your operation or be capable of adapting to it. A town planner can confirm the planning position, and a building consultant can assess condition and compliance. Where a fitout or works are needed, their approval, cost and timing belong in the purchase decision. Business relocation, fitout and construction are not part of a standard buyer’s agency appointment.

Should I buy vacant premises or a property with a tenant in place?

Vacant premises let you occupy at settlement. A tenanted property provides income in the meantime, but you generally cannot occupy until the lease ends or is surrendered by agreement. Buying tenanted can work when you are buying ahead of need and the expiry fits your timing. Check the lease expiry, any options held by the tenant and the rent against your plans, and confirm the finance and tax position with your advisers.

How much can my SMSF spend?

Timing, renewals and your current lease

How early should I start?

Earlier than most occupiers expect. Option dates and notice periods often fall well before expiry, and losing the option removes your main negotiating lever. Where a relocation or fitout is possible, the search, negotiation and works timeline needs to be planned around the expiry rather than compressed into its final months.

Is tenant representation worth it for a renewal?

Renewals are where the information gap is widest. The landlord knows what comparable space is leasing for and what incentives are being offered; most tenants do not. Testing the market before responding to a renewal offer is usually what changes the outcome, whether or not you end up moving. The Lease Renewal vs Relocation Calculator compares the total cost of staying against moving, including fitout, make-good and overlap rent.

What should I check in my current lease before deciding?

The option terms and the window and method for exercising them, any notice required to vacate, the rent review mechanism for the option period, make-good obligations, and any assignment or sublease rights. Options must be exercised exactly as the lease requires, and a late or defective notice can lose the option. For a Queensland retail shop lease, the landlord must give an updated disclosure statement after you exercise an option, and you have a limited period to withdraw. Have your solicitor confirm the dates.

Can I renegotiate my lease mid-term?

You can ask, but the landlord is not obliged to agree unless the lease provides for it. A renegotiation usually works when both sides gain something: a longer term or earlier commitment from the tenant in exchange for a rent adjustment, more or less space, or changes to the review mechanism. The case is strongest when it rests on market evidence and a credible alternative. Common triggers are rent above market, space that no longer fits the operation, or a review the business cannot carry.

Comparing premises and costs

How do I define what premises the business needs?

Start with what the business does, where it needs to be, how much space it needs, what it can carry in rent and costs, and how it expects to change over the lease term. Set the timeline against your option and notice dates. Defining the requirement before the search keeps the comparison on your needs rather than on whatever is currently available, and makes it easier to rule options out quickly.

What is the difference between face rent, effective rent and occupancy cost?

Face rent is the rent stated in the lease. Effective rent spreads the value of any incentive across the term, so it shows what you are really paying. Total occupancy cost adds outgoings, services, fitout, make-good and relocation costs over the term. Headline rents are not comparable until each option is converted to net effective occupancy cost. The Occupancy Cost Comparison Calculator compares up to three premises on that basis.

How are lease incentives usually provided?

As rent-free months, a rent abatement spread across the term, or a contribution to your fitout. Each has a different value over the term and a different effect on cash flow, so compare them on net effective rent rather than on the headline. Check what happens to the incentive if the lease ends early or is assigned. The Lease Incentive & Effective Rent Calculator converts a face rent and incentive into effective rent.

What should I understand about rent reviews and outgoings?

Rent reviews may be fixed percentage increases, CPI adjustments or market reviews, and each shifts risk differently over the term. Outgoings are the property costs you contribute to under the lease, and which ones are recoverable from you depends on its wording. Ask for an outgoings estimate before you commit. Retail shop leases in Queensland carry additional statutory protections around outgoings and reviews. The Rent Review Calculator projects rent year by year under each review type.

Terms, fitout and moving

How long should the lease be, and what security will I need?

Long enough to justify your fitout and relocation costs, with options for flexibility if the business grows or contracts. Assignment and sublease rights matter if plans change. Landlords usually ask for a bank guarantee or bond and sometimes a personal or director guarantee. The amount and the conditions for its return are negotiable and should be settled with the rent and incentive.

Who pays for fitout and make-good?

It depends on what is negotiated. A landlord may contribute to fitout as part of the incentive, and your works will usually need the landlord’s consent and may need building approval. Make-good sets the condition you must return the premises in at the end of the lease. Ask what that will cost and whether the obligation can be capped or scoped now, because it is far harder to negotiate at the end of the lease than at the start.

What happens once terms are agreed, and does this include legal advice?

Agreed terms are recorded in a heads of agreement, then documented in the lease. We advise on the property and commercial terms and coordinate with your solicitor. Your solicitor advises on the legal documents and obligations. For a retail shop lease in Queensland, the landlord must give you a disclosure statement and the proposed lease at least 7 days before you enter into it.

How is the move between premises coordinated?

The lease timetable comes first: when notice must be given on the current premises, when the new premises are available, how long fitout will take, and how long you will pay rent on both. Make-good on the premises you are leaving often runs at the same time as fitout in the new ones. We plan the search and negotiation around those dates, so the expiry does not force a rushed decision.
