Why independent assessment matters

The listing starts the conversation. The evidence informs the purchase.

A listing is prepared to market a property. Before you commit, the same practical questions apply to every purchase. Whichever service you choose, Nick works through them against your requirements and the available evidence.

  • The asking price

    Compare it with recent sales evidence and allow for the works the business needs before it can move in.

  • Vacant possession and timing

    Confirm when the property is available, any existing occupancy and how settlement aligns with your current lease.

  • Suitable premises

    Test access, parking, power, layout and the investigations needed to confirm the property suits your business.

See how information becomes a decision

Under the Full Acquisition Service Nick applies these questions to the properties he sources; under 12-Week Buyer Advisory, to the properties you nominate; under Property Assessment & Negotiation, to the property you have found. They guide the assessment; no assessment can identify every issue with a property.

Commercial property assessment

Illustrative assessment format. Not advice on a real property.

Property: [Example property]
Purpose: [Business premises for owner-occupation]

01Buyer objective and property fit

Information reviewed
[Buyer’s stated purpose, budget range and requirements]
Assessment
Each requirement marked as met, not met or still to confirm.
What it means for the decision
A property that fails a non-negotiable requirement does not progress, whatever the price.

02Operational suitability

Information reviewed
[Site access, loading, parking, power and layout]
Assessment
Whether the premises suit the business’s operations, and what adaptation or fit-out would be needed before occupation.
What it means for the decision
Shows whether the price allows for works needed before the business can operate from the property.

03Price reasoning and comparable evidence

Information reviewed
[Available sales and leasing evidence, with dates and sources]
Assessment
How the asking price or rent compares, and the reasoning for any adjustment between properties.
What it means for the decision
Frames the offer approach and the point at which to walk away.

04Ownership costs and potential capital expenditure

Information reviewed
[Outgoings, building age and condition information]
Assessment
The costs the business carries as owner-occupier and the work the building is likely to need.
What it means for the decision
Shows the full cost of occupation and what capital may need to be set aside.

05Known information, assumptions and verification gaps

Information reviewed
[Documents received and documents still outstanding]
Assessment
Each conclusion marked as known, assumed or still to verify.
What it means for the decision
Assumptions are tested before committing, not after.

06Recommended next step and checks required

Information reviewed
[Summary of the findings above]
Assessment
Proceed, proceed subject to specific checks, or move on.
What it means for the decision
Questions for the selling agent, and the investigations to commission from your solicitor, building consultant or other specialists.

The content and depth of a live assessment depend on the appointed scope and available evidence. Not every service includes a written report in this format. An assessment is not a valuation, building inspection or legal review.

Assessment priorities

The property must work for the business and stand up commercially.

Whichever service you choose, the advice tests both how the premises will work for the business and whether the purchase makes sense against the evidence.

Works for the business

  • Operational suitability for how the business works
  • Access, parking, power and loading
  • Adaptation and fit-out costs
  • Growth capacity
  • Existing lease commitments and timing

Stands up commercially

  • Purchase cost
  • Comparable sales evidence
  • Future leasing and resale flexibility
  • Specialist due diligence coordinated with your solicitor, accountant and other advisers

Common questions

Commercial Buyer Advisory

Which service suits my situation?

It depends on how involved you want Nick to be and where your search is up to. If you want Nick to lead the search, assessment and negotiation, the Full Acquisition Service fits. If you prefer to search yourself with advice, 12-Week Buyer Advisory fits. If you have already found a property, Property Assessment & Negotiation fits. The complimentary initial discussion confirms the appropriate service.

Can Nick manage the entire acquisition?

Yes. Under the Full Acquisition Service, Nick leads the search, assessment and negotiation, coordinating the acquisition alongside your solicitor and relevant advisers. Scope and fees are agreed before appointment.

Can I search myself with advice from Nick?

Yes. 12-Week Buyer Advisory is for business owners running their own premises search. You find and inspect properties, deal with selling agents and submit offers. Nick helps set your buying criteria, reviews the properties you nominate and advises on your next move. Scope and fees are agreed before appointment.

What if I have already found a property?

Property Assessment & Negotiation covers a property you have already found. Nick assesses the opportunity and represents you in the negotiation under an agreed appointment. An ongoing property search is a separate service.

What happens after an offer is accepted?

It depends on the appointed service. Under 12-Week Buyer Advisory, Nick’s role is advisory guidance on your next steps. Under the Full Acquisition Service, Nick coordinates the acquisition alongside your solicitor and relevant advisers. Your appointment sets out Represent’s involvement after offer acceptance, including any agreed assessment, coordination or advisory support. Legal and other specialist advice remains with your appointed advisers.

How are scope and fees agreed?

Scope and fees are agreed before appointment. The initial discussion is complimentary and establishes your requirements and the appropriate service. Property assessments, advice and negotiation begin only under a separately agreed appointment.

Which separate professional advisers may be required?

Depending on the purchase, that may include your solicitor, lender or finance broker, accountant or tax adviser, valuer, town planner and building consultant. Legal, finance, tax, valuation, planning and building advice remain with the relevant specialists. Nick provides the agreed commercial assessment and advice.
