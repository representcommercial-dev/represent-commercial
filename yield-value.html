How this is calculated

Net income

Gross rent plus outgoings recovered from tenants, less the property’s total outgoings, less an allowance for vacancy and non-recovery applied to the gross rent. Where recoveries fall short of outgoings, the shortfall reduces net income — which is how a gross lease, a vacancy or a non-recoverable land tax assessment shows up in value.

Net yield

Net income divided by the price. The gross yield shown alongside it is gross rent divided by the price, which is why the two diverge as outgoings rise.

Yield on total cost

Net income divided by the price plus acquisition costs. It is the yield actually earned on the money committed, rather than on the contract price.

Property value

In value mode the net income is capitalised at the market yield you enter: value equals net income divided by the yield. The result is an indicative capitalised figure, not a valuation.

Sensitivity

The tables hold income constant and move the yield, then hold the yield constant and move income, in even steps either side of your figures. A quarter of a percent on the yield moves value more than most buyers expect, which is why yield evidence carries so much weight in a negotiation.

What is excluded

Capital expenditure, incentives, letting-up allowances, over-rented or under-rented positions, lease expiry risk, depreciation, GST and taxation are not modelled. Two assets on the same net income can justify materially different yields.

The yield is an argument, not a fact.
