How this is calculated

Total cost to acquire

The purchase price plus transfer duty, legal and conveyancing, due diligence, advisory fees, finance establishment costs and any capital works required immediately. It is the money committed to own the asset, which is the only sensible denominator for a return.

Yield on price and yield on total cost

Net income divided by the price, and net income divided by the total cost. The gap between the two is the acquisition costs, and on a Queensland commercial purchase it routinely removes 30 to 40 basis points from the headline yield.

Cash on cash

Net income less interest, divided by the equity committed. Interest is charged on an interest-only basis at the rate entered; principal repayments, loan amortisation and any interest capitalised during works are not modelled.

Interest cover

Net income divided by the annual interest cost. Lenders test this covenant alongside loan to value, and a thin cover ratio constrains gearing well before the loan to value limit binds.

Transfer duty

Calculated on the purchase price at the standard Queensland transfer duty rates: nil to $5,000; $1.50 per $100 above $5,000 to $75,000; $1,050 plus $3.50 per $100 above $75,000 to $540,000; $17,325 plus $4.50 per $100 above $540,000 to $1,000,000; and $38,025 plus $5.75 per $100 above $1,000,000. The calculation assumes the purchase price is the dutiable value and that no concession, exemption or surcharge applies. Additional foreign acquirer duty, landholder duty and any concession change the result, so duty must be confirmed with your solicitor. You can override the figure and enter an assessed amount instead.

What is excluded

GST and the margin scheme, land tax on an aggregated holding, income tax, depreciation, rental growth, incentive amortisation, vacancy allowances and disposal costs are not modelled.
