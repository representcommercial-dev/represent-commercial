How this is calculated

Passing and market income

Both are area multiplied by the relevant net rent, less the non-recoverable outgoings you enter. The market rent is grown at the rate you set until the reversion date, so a reversion two years out is assessed on the market rent expected then, not on today’s.

Value

Each income position is capitalised at the market net yield: value equals net income divided by the yield. The uplift is the difference between the two, less the cost to capture the market rent.

Cost to capture

Incentive, fitout contribution or capital works needed to let at the market rent. It is deducted from the reversionary value in full rather than amortised, which is the conservative treatment.

Reversion percentage

How far the passing rent sits below the market rent today, as a percentage of market. It is the figure that determines whether an asset is genuinely under-rented or merely priced on optimistic evidence.

What is excluded

The time value of money, vacancy risk between expiry and reletting, letting fees, transaction costs, tax, capital expenditure beyond the cost to capture, and any yield shift between now and reversion are not modelled. A reversion is only realisable if the market rent is real and the tenant or a replacement will pay it.

A reversion is a thesis, not a number.

Whether it is capturable depends on the lease, the tenant, the building and the depth of leasing demand at the rent being assumed. Represent tests that before a buyer pays for it.
