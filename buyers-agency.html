What goes wrong

Where commercial acquisitions come unstuck

Every property is presented by someone acting for the seller. These are the six ways buyers get caught.

01

Paying too much

The yield looks acceptable, but the passing rent is above market or the outgoings recovery is weaker than assumed. Both inflate the price you are paying.

02

Buying the wrong property

A sound asset that does not suit the business or the investment objective: wrong location, wrong configuration, wrong lease profile, wrong hold period.

03

Due-diligence risks found late

Make-good exposure, unapproved works, contamination, non-recoverable outgoings, capital items falling due, a tenant weaker than the covenant suggests.

04

Negotiating from a weak position

Without evidence and without a walk-away price, negotiation becomes a conversation about the vendor's expectation rather than the property's value.

05

Missing the better alternative

Portfolio decisions made on what happened to be listed that month. Off-market stock and owners open to an approach never enter the comparison.

06

Buying when leasing was better

For an owner-occupier, purchase and lease need to be compared on total cost and flexibility before capital is committed. Often it is not compared at all.

The acquisition process

BRIEF · SEARCH · ANALYSE · ACQUIRE

01 · BRIEF

Brief

What the property has to do for the business or the portfolio. Criteria, precincts, budget, structure and walk-away parameters agreed in writing.

02 · SEARCH

Search + shortlist

On-market, pre-market and off-market coverage, including approaches to owners not currently selling. You see a shortlist, with the reasons the rest were excluded.

03 · ANALYSE

Analysis + due diligence

Income, lease profile, market rent, outgoings recovery, comparable evidence and capital exposure. Pre-purchase due diligence coordinated with your solicitor and consultants.

04 · ACQUIRE

Negotiate + settle

Price and terms negotiated from evidence and a defined walk-away position, then the transaction managed through to settlement.

Common questions

Buying commercial property in Queensland

What does a commercial buyer’s agent do?

Acts only for the buyer: setting the brief and criteria, searching on-market and off-market, shortlisting, analysing income, lease profile and market rent, coordinating pre-purchase due diligence, negotiating price and terms, and supporting you through to settlement.

How do I know if I am paying too much?

By testing the asking price against comparable sale evidence, the passing rent against market rent, and the outgoings recovery against what the lease actually permits. A price can look acceptable on yield and still be too high if the rent sits above market or the recovery position is weaker than assumed.

Do you act for owner-occupiers as well as investors?

Yes. Investments are assessed on income, lease quality and value-add potential. Owner-occupier acquisitions are assessed against the business requirement as well as the investment fundamentals, including the option of continuing to lease instead of buying. See buyer’s agency for investors and for owner-occupiers.

How much does a commercial buyer’s agent cost?

Fees depend on the acquisition brief and the scope of work. Before appointment, we agree the services, fee basis, payment stages and any minimum fee in writing. The initial acquisition brief discussion is free and does not appoint Represent Commercial.

Can you help if I have already found a property?

Yes. Tell Nick which property you are considering and where the transaction stands. We can discuss a scope covering assessment, comparable evidence, due diligence coordination and negotiation, with the services and fees agreed before work begins.

Do you act for sellers?

No. Represent Commercial does not act for vendors and does not run vendor sales campaigns. That is what makes the advice on the buy side worth having.
