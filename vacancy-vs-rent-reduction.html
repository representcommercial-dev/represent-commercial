How this is calculated

Net income

Rent is accrued month by month from the date the lease commences to the end of the period assessed, escalating at the annual review on each anniversary of commencement. Outgoings are charged for every month the tenancy sits empty, and the letting fee is charged once on the first year’s net rent.

Why vacancy costs more than the rent

An empty tenancy earns nothing and still incurs rates, land tax, insurance and upkeep. The months lost also shorten the income period, so the higher rent is collected for fewer months than the lower one.

When waiting falls behind

The first whole month of vacancy at which holding out produces less modelled net income than accepting the lower rent after the downtime entered. The exact break-even point may sit between whole months. This is an income comparison, not a complete leasing recommendation.

Vacancy inputs are rounded to whole months. The assessment period is rounded to whole years, between one and 25 years. Both leases are assumed to continue to the end of that period. Amounts exclude GST.

Break-even rent

The rent that, after the downtime entered for the lower-rent option, produces the same modelled net income as holding out for the target. A higher rent produces more income on these assumptions.

What is excluded

Incentives, rent-free periods, fitout contributions, marketing costs, the time value of money, tenant covenant and the effect of a lower passing rent on the asset’s capital value are not modelled. A lower rent locked in for a long term carries a valuation consequence beyond the income comparison.

A lower rent can leave an owner ahead

This illustrative example compares a 500 m² tenancy over three years from today. The target is $200/m² net with three months of vacancy. The alternative is $190/m² net with one month of downtime. Vacancy outgoings are $40/m² p.a., the letting fee is 15% of first-year net rent in both options, and annual reviews are set to zero to make the comparison easy to follow. These are example inputs, not market rental evidence or a fee quote.

A lease is an asset decision

The calculation gives you a starting point. Before changing the asking rent, consider:

  • Evidence: comparable premises, competing stock, enquiry quality and inspection feedback. Weak enquiry may reflect presentation, access or suitability as well as price.
  • The tenant: ability to pay, proposed use, security and the risk of needing to relet.
  • The whole deal: incentives, fitout contributions, lease term, reviews, options and the costs you can recover.
  • The asset strategy: how the lease supports future sale, refinancing, refurbishment or redevelopment.

A lower rent with a stronger tenant may be attractive. A lower rent with substantial incentives or weak security may not be. Test the numbers, then assess the lease.
