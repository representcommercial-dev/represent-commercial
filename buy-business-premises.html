Before you buy

Why use a commercial buyer’s agent?

  • The selling agent represents the seller

    The agent marketing a property is engaged by the vendor. Nick is engaged by your business and negotiates on your behalf.

  • Finding a listing is only part of it

    Whether premises suit the business also depends on access, layout, services and how the operation will run day to day.

  • Price, running costs and works belong together

    The purchase price needs to be weighed with operating costs and any works the premises need before the business can use them.

Buying with a tenant in place

Buy ahead of your move.

If your business does not need to move immediately, a tenanted property may be an option. Rent may offset some holding costs while you plan for future occupation. Your solicitor should confirm when vacant possession can be obtained, including renewal options and notice requirements.

  1. Match the lease expiry to your move

    Look for a lease that ends close to when your business needs the space. Expiry alone does not confirm vacant possession, so allow time for make-good, fit-out and a fallback plan.

  2. Read the lease before you price it

    A validly exercised option to renew usually binds both parties to a further term. Have your solicitor check options, termination rights, notice requirements and make-good terms before relying on any possession date.

  3. Plan finance and tax with your advisers

    A tenanted purchase can be treated differently from buying vacant premises. Your lender, accountant and solicitor advise on finance, GST and duty.

Common questions

Before you appoint a buyer’s agent.

Can we buy a property with a tenant and move in later?

It may be possible, depending on the lease. Nick can look for tenanted property with a lease ending close to when your business needs to move, and review options to renew, termination rights, notice requirements and make-good terms that could affect possession. Lease expiry alone does not confirm vacant possession: your solicitor should confirm when it can be obtained. Your lender, accountant and solicitor should advise on finance, GST and duty for a tenanted purchase.

Can Nick help if we’ve already found a property?

Yes. Nick can assess the property against your brief and sales evidence, flag issues for your advisers to investigate and negotiate the purchase under an agreed scope.

How are scope and fees agreed?

Fees depend on the acquisition brief and the scope of work. Before appointment, we agree the services, fee basis, payment stages and any minimum fee in writing. The initial discussion is complimentary and does not appoint Represent Commercial.

How long can a commercial purchase take?

It varies. The search depends on how specific the brief is and what comes to market. Once a contract is signed, the due diligence, finance and settlement periods are set by the contract, and fit-out can add further time. Your solicitor advises on the contract terms.

Does Nick work alongside our accountant, broker and solicitor?

Yes. We work alongside your existing advisers so the property search and transaction reflect the requirements they establish. Represent provides property acquisition services, rather than financial, tax, legal or credit advice.

What if we’re not sure buying is the right decision?

Start with the initial discussion. Nick can talk through how long the business expects to need the space, the cash a purchase would tie up and the flexibility you want to keep. Your accountant should advise on the financial and tax position.

Can we buy through an SMSF?

Raise this with your qualified SMSF, finance and legal advisers early. Once they confirm the relevant requirements, Nick can factor them into the property brief and search.
