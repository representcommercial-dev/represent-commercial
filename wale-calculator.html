How this is calculated

WALE by income

Each tenancy’s years remaining to expiry is multiplied by its annual net rent. Those weighted terms are added together and divided by the total annual net rent. A tenancy paying half the income therefore carries half the weight, which is why one major tenant can set the number almost on its own.

Years remaining

The number of days from the calculation date to the lease expiry date, divided by 365.25. A lease that has already expired is treated as zero rather than a negative, so a holding-over tenancy does not flatter the result by reducing it below what the remaining leases support.

Expiry profile

Income is allocated to a band by the time remaining on its lease and expressed as a share of total passing income. The profile is what reveals concentration — a respectable WALE can still sit on a single expiry date.

Options to renew

Enter the expiry of the current term. Market practice is to calculate WALE to the earliest expiry and treat further options as upside, because an option sits with the tenant, not the owner. Where an option has been exercised and documented, use the extended expiry.

What is excluded

Area-weighted WALE, vacancy, incentives, rent reviews, early termination and make-good are not modelled, and no assessment of tenant covenant is made. A long WALE to a weak covenant is not a long WALE.

A WALE is built, not measured.

Expiry dates get staggered, terms get lengthened and covenants get tested through active leasing decisions made years before the numbers matter. Represent manages and leases commercial property with the expiry profile in view.
