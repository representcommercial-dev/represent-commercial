How to change commercial property managers in Brisbane

If calls go unanswered, lease events drift or reporting leaves you guessing, changing managers may be worth considering. Start with the appointment and the handover plan, so the property keeps operating while responsibility changes.

Nick O'TooleFounder & Director, Represent Commercial
Published 2 November 2026
Direct answer

Where should you start?

Start by reviewing your current management appointment, confirming how it can be ended and agreeing the incoming manager’s scope. Then coordinate the records, funds, security, critical dates and tenant communication around a clear changeover date. The tenant’s lease and the agent’s management appointment are separate agreements.

Before giving notice

Write down what is failing and what you expect the next manager to do differently. A missed rent review, repeated arrears, unexplained expenditure and poor communication need different remedies. Ask whether the issue is administration, strategy or both.

Obtain the signed management appointment and any variations. Check the services, term, termination provisions, notice requirements, fees and any separate leasing authority. Do not assume residential management notice rules apply to a commercial appointment. If the position is disputed or unclear, have your solicitor confirm it before notice is issued.

Queensland’s Office of Fair Trading identifies Form 6A as the commercial agent appointment form and requires the written appointment to record the agreed services and payment terms. See the official appointment guidance.

Agree the new scope and fees

Compare what each manager actually includes. A percentage alone does not show who handles rent reviews, renewals, outgoings reconciliation, inspections or vacancy planning. Confirm which services carry additional fees, how expenditure is authorised and what reporting you will receive.

Ask who will make the decisions and who you will speak with when a tenant stops paying or an expiry approaches. At Represent Commercial, Nick O’Toole leads the appointment personally. Our commercial property management service sets out the available scope.

The commercial management handover checklist

Agree who is responsible for each item, when it will arrive and how it will be checked:

  • Lease records: signed leases, variations, disclosure documents where relevant, side agreements and correspondence.
  • Financial position: tenant ledgers, arrears, invoices, owner statements, approved expenditure, outgoings budgets and reconciliation records.
  • Funds and security: trust balances, deposits, bonds and original bank guarantees, including their custody and expiry details. Confirm the correct transfer process for each item.
  • Critical dates: reviews, options, expiries, insurance renewals and outstanding tenant commitments.
  • Property operations: keys, access controls, contractor contacts, maintenance history, compliance records and open work orders.

Reconcile the opening position against the outgoing manager’s closing records. A file transfer is only useful when the information is complete and the balances agree.

Keep tenants informed

Agree the effective date and who will tell tenants about the change. The notice should explain the new contact, maintenance and payment arrangements clearly. Coordinate the final invoice from the outgoing manager with the first invoice from the incoming manager to avoid duplicate demands.

Tenants should independently verify changed bank details through a trusted contact before paying. Changing managers does not itself renegotiate the rent, lease term or other agreed tenant obligations.

What should happen after the handover?

The first review should confirm rent and arrears, the next lease events, security held, outstanding maintenance and any unreconciled outgoings. Agree a short list of priorities with dates and responsibility, rather than treating the handover as complete when the keys arrive.

If the issue is strategic oversight while your existing manager continues the administration, commercial asset management may be the more suitable scope. If you want an independent starting point, request a free written Property Performance Review.

Questions owners ask

Can I change managers during a tenant’s lease?

A tenant’s lease is separate from the management appointment. A change can be possible during the lease, subject to properly ending the current appointment and arranging the handover. Check your own agreement before committing to a date.

Do I need to contact the outgoing manager myself?

Once appointed, Represent Commercial can prepare the proposed notice for you to sign and coordinate the handover with your current manager. Any disputed contractual issue should be referred to your solicitor.

How long does a change take?

The timetable depends on your appointment, agreed notice, record availability and open issues. Confirm the dates after those matters are checked rather than relying on a standard turnaround.
