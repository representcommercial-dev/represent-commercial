What is a Commercial Property Performance Review?

A Commercial Property Performance Review is an owner-side assessment of the factors affecting a commercial property’s current income, leasing position, lease risk, expenditure and longer-term performance.

Represent Commercial uses the review to identify key findings, immediate risks, potential opportunities, important information gaps and practical priorities for the property.

Scope

What does the Property Performance Review examine?

The review considers the property across ten areas. Not every area applies equally to every property. The weight given to each depends on the asset, its tenancy position and the decisions the owner is facing.

01

Income & recoverability

Current income, outgoings recovery and potential income leakage.

02

Lease position

Lease expiries, options, rent reviews and significant upcoming lease events.

03

Tenant & covenant

Tenant quality, concentration and relevant occupancy risks.

04

Leasing & vacancy

Vacancy exposure, leasing position, competing stock, market positioning and potential downtime.

05

Property presentation

Presentation, available property information and potential leasing or occupation friction.

06

Maintenance & expenditure

Responsibilities, expenditure controls, recurring issues and near-term priorities.

07

Capital works

Known or potential improvements requiring consideration or further investigation.

08

Management & reporting

Quality of information, reporting, accountability and potential management weaknesses.

09

Asset positioning

Issues that may affect longer-term income resilience, future leasing and the property’s market position.

10

Important unknowns

Information that should be obtained, clarified or verified before major decisions are made.

Deliverable

What will you receive?

An initial owner-side assessment of the property. Where relevant to the property, it covers the items listed.

Recommendations are based on the information available and the circumstances of the property. Further investigation or specialist advice may be required before particular decisions are made.

The review is not a valuation, building inspection, engineering report, or legal, tax or financial advice.

Timing

When should an owner consider a Property Performance Review?

A review is useful before a significant decision, whether or not the property is underperforming. Common circumstances include:

  • The property is vacant or taking longer than expected to lease.
  • A lease expiry or option is approaching.
  • You are uncertain whether rent is appropriately positioned.
  • You are concerned about tenant or covenant risk.
  • Outgoings or operating costs need closer examination.
  • Maintenance expenditure is becoming reactive.
  • Capital works are being considered.
  • Existing reporting does not provide enough visibility.
  • You are considering changing property manager or leasing agent.
  • You have recently acquired the property.
  • You want a clearer asset plan before making another significant property decision.

Owner-side proposition

Property management is not rent collection. It is the execution layer of the owner’s asset strategy.

A manager can process invoices, collect rent and respond to maintenance requests. The more important question is whether each decision supports the owner’s wider commercial objectives.

Every rent review, maintenance request, lease negotiation, operating expense and tenant conversation can affect income, risk and the future position of the asset. See how we approach commercial property management.

The management model

What can administration-only property management miss?

When management is treated purely as administration, decisions tend to be made one at a time. A maintenance approval, a rent review or a lease variation can each be handled correctly in isolation and still work against the owner’s wider plan for the property.

Isolated decisions can affect:

  • Lease expiry exposure
  • Recoverability
  • Income leakage
  • Tenant quality
  • Downtime
  • Incentives
  • Maintenance costs
  • Capital expenditure
  • Future reletting
  • Property presentation
  • Owner visibility
  • Asset positioning

What I Would Look At First

I wouldn’t assume the answer is to reduce the rent, spend more on marketing, change managers or carry out works.

I would first establish what the owner is trying to achieve, what the property is currently producing, the lease and tenant position, what decisions are approaching, what the market evidence tells us and what important information is missing.

Only then would I decide which issues deserve action.

Do not prescribe before diagnosing.

Leasing and management

A lease is an asset decision.

Leasing and property management should not operate as separate functions. Tenant selection, incentives, rent reviews, recoverability, options, covenant quality and lease expiry all influence the future performance of the property.

The review looks at the leasing position alongside management and expenditure. Read more about our approach to commercial property leasing, or the full cost of leasing out a commercial property.

Common questions

Questions owners ask

What is a Commercial Property Performance Review?

It is an owner-side assessment of the factors affecting a commercial property’s income, leasing position, lease risk, expenditure and longer-term performance. Represent Commercial uses it to identify key findings, immediate risks, potential opportunities, information gaps and practical priorities. The scope depends on the property and the information available.

How do I know if my commercial property is underperforming?

Underperformance is relative to the owner’s objectives and the market. Useful signs to examine include rent compared with current market evidence, vacancy duration, outgoings that are not being recovered, a concentrated lease expiry profile, rising reactive maintenance and reporting that does not explain what is happening. None of these alone proves a problem. They indicate where to look.

What should I review before a commercial lease expires?

Review the tenant’s likely intentions, any option terms and exercise dates, current market rent evidence, the incentives competing properties are offering, make-good obligations, the condition and presentation of the space, and the downtime and cost if the tenant leaves. Starting well before the expiry or option window leaves more choices available.

Should I change commercial property managers?

Only once you understand what is and is not working. A change can be appropriate where reporting, decision-making or accountability falls short of what the asset needs. It can also be unnecessary where the issue is market conditions, pricing or the property itself. The review considers the current position, management approach and market context before recommending any major change.

Should I reduce the rent if my commercial property isn’t leasing?

Not automatically. Rent is one factor among several, including presentation, the quality of property information, marketing reach, lease terms, incentive structure and competing stock. A reduction can be the right decision, but it is worth confirming that rent is the actual barrier first. The vacancy vs rent reduction calculator compares the two.

How can vacancy affect a commercial property?

Vacancy removes rental income and usually leaves the owner carrying outgoings that would otherwise be recovered. Re-leasing can then involve incentives, leasing fees and possible works. A vacancy can also affect how the property is assessed for financing or sale. The cost to lease guide sets out these costs in more detail.

Who pays commercial property outgoings while a property is vacant?

Generally the owner. Outgoings attributable to a vacant tenancy usually cannot be passed on to other tenants, so the owner bears them until the space is leased. The exact position depends on the lease terms, the type of property and the legislation that applies.

How often should a commercial property strategy be reviewed?

At least annually, and before any significant event such as a lease expiry, option, rent review, capital works decision, refinancing or change of manager. Market conditions and tenant circumstances change, so a strategy set at acquisition may not still fit.

Is a Property Performance Review a valuation?

No. It is not a formal valuation, building inspection, engineering report, or legal, tax or financial advice. Where one of those is needed before a decision, the review will say so.

Do I need to change property managers to request a review?

No. The purpose of the Review is to understand the property’s current position before recommending a major change. The appropriate outcome may be to continue the existing strategy, investigate a particular issue, change an aspect of the approach or take no immediate action.
