CGT TREATMENT calculator.

For a commercial property acquired on 1 July 2027, this illustrates the difference between the former CGT treatment (50% discount) and the legislated rules (CPI cost-base indexation, taxed at marginal rate with a 30% minimum floor).

⚠ Illustration only — confirm the application to your circumstances with your accountant Based on the legislated rules applying to relevant gains accruing from 1 July 2027. An asset purchased on 1 July 2027 falls entirely under the new regime (the pre/post split-valuation transitional rules apply only to assets bought before that date). New regime: the cost base is indexed by CPI over the hold period, and only the real gain above inflation is taxed — at the 47% marginal rate, subject to a 30% minimum floor on the real gain. Former treatment (shown for comparison): 50% discount on the nominal gain, taxed at 47%. This is an illustration, not tax advice. The relevant rules have been legislated and apply to relevant gains accruing from 1 July 2027. Tax treatment depends on individual circumstances and should be confirmed with an appropriately qualified adviser. For simplicity, this calculation does not include any rent, income, or holding expenses — it models the capital gain and CGT on sale only. It also does not include purchase costs or any other factor that impacts cost base (e.g. capex, depreciation etc).

Their application to a particular gain depends on the taxpayer, the asset and the transitional provisions. The model applies a simplified calculation: sale value compounds purchase price at the growth rate; the new-regime cost base is indexed at CPI; tax is the real gain × 47% (the 30% floor does not bind at a 47% marginal rate). It ignores capital works deductions, other cost-base adjustments, carried losses, entity structure, super-fund or company treatment, small-business concessions, and every other factor affecting a real liability. Note that super funds and companies are treated differently under the reform. CGT is a matter for a qualified accountant or tax adviser.
