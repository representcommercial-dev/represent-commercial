Commercial Property Leasing

How much does it cost to lease out a commercial property?

Leasing a commercial property can involve advertising, photography, floor plans, leasing commission, incentives and other costs before the property starts producing rent again.

But those costs shouldn't be considered in isolation. The more important question is:

“What is the total cost of getting the property leased well?”

Direct answer

What does it cost to lease a commercial property?

There is no single fixed cost to lease a commercial property. Owners may incur leasing commission, advertising, professional photography, floor plans, incentives and other campaign expenses. The total cost also includes the financial effect of vacancy, such as lost rent, unrecovered outgoings and ongoing holding costs.

The appropriate amount depends on the property, market, lease terms and leasing strategy.

The question is not simply: “Why does it cost so much to lease my property?”

It is: “What is the total cost of getting the property leased well?”

Cost breakdown

Where does the money actually go?

01

Leasing commission

Commercial leasing agents generally charge a fee when a new lease is successfully negotiated. The calculation method varies between agency appointments, so owners should understand exactly what their commission covers and how it is calculated.

02

Property advertising

Commercial property portals offer different advertising packages and levels of exposure. More exposure can help, but paying for a larger advertising package does not automatically solve problems with pricing, presentation or positioning.

03

Professional photography

Photography should help prospective tenants understand the actual property being offered. Generic, outdated or poorly selected photographs can create unnecessary uncertainty.

04

Floor plans and property information

Useful dimensions, floor plans, access information, parking, outgoings and practical property information can help tenants determine whether an inspection is worthwhile.

05

Tenant incentives

Depending on the property and market, lease negotiations may involve rent-free periods, fitout contributions or other incentives.

06 · Vacancy

THE COST OF vacancy.

Every additional week of unnecessary vacancy can mean:

  • Lost rent
  • Unrecovered outgoings
  • Ongoing holding costs
  • Delayed income
  • Potentially additional marketing expenditure
“The biggest leasing cost may not be the agent's fee. It may be unnecessary vacancy.”
The real problem

Why does it cost so much to lease my commercial property?

Sometimes the problem isn't simply that leasing costs too much.

The problem is that money is being spent without first identifying why the property isn't leasing.

A property can have premium advertising and still underperform.

Before spending more money, reducing the rent or changing agents, an owner should understand where the friction actually sits.

Sensitivity

What changes the leasing outcome?

The cost of leasing a property is not fixed. It moves with the property, the market and how the campaign is run. These are the factors that most often change the result.

Asking rent

Whether the rent is supported by the market and by competing available stock.

Competing stock

What else a tenant can lease nearby, and on what terms.

Presentation

Whether the listing, photographs and property information help a tenant decide an inspection is worthwhile.

Occupancy-cost transparency

Whether a tenant can understand what the premises will actually cost to occupy.

Enquiry and inspection

How quickly enquiries are answered and how easily inspections are arranged.

Incentives

What the market expects, and how any incentive affects the effective rent.

Advertising exposure

Wider exposure can help, but it does not fix pricing, presentation or positioning.

Inspection feedback

Whether what tenants say after inspecting is used to adjust the campaign.

Property condition

Works a tenant would need before occupying, and who is expected to fund them.

Market conditions

Demand for this type of space at the time the property is offered.

Key insightEach factor calls for a different response, which is why diagnosis comes before more spending.

WHAT I WOULD look at.

Before recommending that an owner spends more on advertising, reduces the rent or changes agents, I would want to understand where the friction actually sits.

Is the rent right for this property now?

I compare the asking rent with what competing properties are offering, including incentives, rather than with what the property achieved last time.

What does a tenant see first?

I look at the listing as a tenant would: the photographs, the floor plan and the information that decides whether an inspection is worth making.

Can a tenant work out the real cost?

I check whether outgoings and the likely occupancy cost are clear. Uncertainty about cost gives a tenant a reason to move on to the next listing.

What happens after an enquiry?

I want to know how quickly enquiries are answered, how easily inspections are arranged and what the tenant hears afterwards.

What is the feedback telling us?

Inspection feedback is evidence. I want to see whether it has changed the campaign, the presentation or the terms.

Is the spend in the right place?

Before recommending more advertising or a lower rent, I want to know which problem it is meant to solve.

Common questions

Commercial leasing costs

How much do commercial leasing agents charge?

Commercial leasing agents generally charge a fee when a new lease is successfully negotiated. How that fee is calculated varies between agency appointments, so there is no single standard rate. Owners should confirm in writing what the commission covers, how it is calculated and when it becomes payable.

Who pays to advertise a commercial property for lease?

Advertising is usually an owner cost, agreed as part of the agency appointment. The amount and what it includes depend on the campaign agreed between the owner and the agent, so owners should approve a marketing budget before the campaign starts.

What marketing costs are involved in leasing commercial property?

Marketing costs can include portal advertising, professional photography, floor plans, signage and property information material. Which of these are needed, and at what level, depends on the property and the tenants it is likely to attract.

How much does commercial property vacancy cost?

Vacancy costs the rent that is not being received, plus outgoings the owner cannot recover from a tenant and other holding costs such as finance. The amount depends on the rent, the outgoings and how long the property stays vacant. Each additional week of unnecessary vacancy adds to it.

Should I spend more advertising a vacant commercial property?

Sometimes. More exposure can help, but a larger advertising package does not solve problems with asking rent, presentation, missing information or slow responses to enquiry. It is worth identifying why the property is not leasing before increasing the budget.

Why isn't my commercial property leasing?

Common causes include an asking rent the market does not support, stronger competing stock, a listing that does not communicate the property's advantages, missing property or occupancy-cost information, and friction in answering enquiries or arranging inspections. Each requires a different response.

Should I change commercial leasing agents?

Not necessarily. Changing agents makes sense if the campaign has a problem the current agent is not addressing. It does not help if the underlying issue is the rent, the property or the market. An independent review can help separate those questions before you decide.
