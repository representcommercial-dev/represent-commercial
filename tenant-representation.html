Why occupiers engage us

The situation is usually one of these

Every property you inspect is represented by someone acting for the landlord. These are the points at which that matters most.

You need new premises

Growth, a new site or a first lease. The requirement needs to be defined before the search, or the search defines the requirement for you.

You are considering relocating

Moving has costs the rent comparison does not show: fitout, make-good, downtime, churn. The comparison has to be on total cost.

Your lease is up for renewal

The renewal offer arrives with no market context. Without evidence, the only available response is to accept or to guess.

You want to renegotiate mid-term

Rent above market, space no longer fitting the operation, or a review mechanism producing a result the business cannot carry.

You cannot compare the options properly

Different areas, incentives, outgoings and review structures. Headline rents are not comparable until they are converted to net effective occupancy cost.

The expiry is close

Options lapse, notice periods pass and leverage disappears with them. Pressure is the landlord's strongest negotiating position.

The engagement

Brief · Search · Compare · Negotiate

  1. 01 · BRIEF

    Brief

    What the business does, where it needs to be, how much space, what it can carry and how it expects to change. Timeline set against option and notice dates.

  2. 02 · SEARCH

    Search + shortlist

    On-market and off-market canvass, including space not advertised. Shortlist and inspections arranged and attended, with the incumbent premises kept in the comparison.

  3. 03 · COMPARE

    Occupancy cost review

    Every option converted to net effective occupancy cost: rent, incentive, outgoings, fitout, make-good and relocation cost over the term, side by side.

  4. 04 · NEGOTIATE

    Negotiate + document

    Competing options held open as leverage. Rent, incentive, term, reviews and make-good negotiated tenant-side, then documentation managed through to occupation.

What Represent Commercial negotiates

Negotiate the deal. Not just the rent.

Finding available space is only the beginning. Six questions test whether a deal is worth signing.

Rent evidenceWhat have comparable tenancies actually agreed? What is the net effective rent once incentives and outgoings are counted?
IncentiveHow is it delivered: rent-free, abatement or fitout contribution? What is it actually worth over the term?
Term + flexibilityWhat happens if the business grows, contracts or relocates? What options, assignment and sublease rights apply?
Occupancy costWhat will outgoings, services, fitout and relocation cost across the term, and which of them sit with the tenant?
Make-goodWhat condition must the premises be returned in, what will that cost, and can the obligation be capped or scoped now?
CompetitionWhat matters to the landlord besides face rent? Where is the tenant's walk-away point?

Common questions

Tenant representation in Brisbane

What is tenant representation?

A tenant representative acts only for the occupier: defining the requirement, searching on-market and off-market, shortlisting and inspecting, comparing options on total occupancy cost rather than headline rent, negotiating rent, incentive and term, and managing documentation through to occupation. The Occupancy Cost Comparison Calculator compares up to three premises on effective annual cost, net of incentives and fitout.

Is it worth it for a renewal?

Renewals are where the information gap is widest. The landlord knows what comparable space is leasing for and what incentives are being offered; most tenants do not. Testing the market before responding to a renewal offer is usually what changes the outcome, whether or not you end up moving. The Lease Renewal vs Relocation Calculator compares the total cost of staying against moving, including fitout, make-good and overlap rent.

Who pays the fee?

Fee structures vary by engagement and are agreed in writing before work begins. Some engagements are fee-for-service to the tenant; in others the fee is met by the landlord. Either way the arrangement is disclosed at the outset.

How early should I start?

Earlier than most occupiers expect. Option dates and notice periods often fall well before expiry, and losing the option removes your main negotiating lever. Where a relocation or fitout is possible, the search, negotiation and works timeline needs to be planned around the expiry rather than compressed into its final months.
