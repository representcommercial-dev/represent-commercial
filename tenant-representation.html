Why occupiers engage us

The situation is usually one of these

Every property you inspect is represented by someone acting for the landlord. These are the points at which that matters most.

01

You need new premises

Growth, a new site or a first lease. The requirement needs to be defined before the search, or the search defines the requirement for you.

02

You are considering relocating

Moving has costs the rent comparison does not show — fitout, make-good, downtime, churn. The comparison has to be on total cost.

03

Your lease is up for renewal

The renewal offer arrives with no market context. Without evidence, the only available response is to accept or to guess.

04

You want to renegotiate mid-term

Rent above market, space no longer fitting the operation, or a review mechanism producing a result the business cannot carry.

05

You cannot compare the options properly

Different areas, incentives, outgoings and review structures. Headline rents are not comparable until they are converted to net effective occupancy cost.

06

Incentives are opaque

Rent-free, fitout contribution and abatement are negotiable and rarely volunteered. You need to know what is currently being offered in that market.

07

The expiry is close

Options lapse, notice periods pass and leverage disappears with them. Pressure is the landlord's strongest negotiating position.

08

The landlord knows more than you do

They have transacted this market repeatedly this year. For most occupiers, it is once every five years.

Leasing judgement

ACTIVE NEGOTIATION. NOT PASSIVE rent comparison.

Finding available space is only the beginning. The greater value lies in knowing what to test, what assumptions to challenge and where a proposal costs more than it first appears. Six questions test whether a deal is worth signing.

01
Rent evidence

The rent is in line with the market.

What have comparable tenancies actually agreed? What is the net effective rent once incentives and outgoings are counted?

Benchmark / Evidence / Offer
02
Incentive

The incentive is generous.

How is it delivered — rent-free, abatement or fitout contribution? What is it actually worth over the term?

Value / Timing / Structure
03
Term + flexibility

It is a standard five-year term.

What happens if the business grows, contracts or relocates? What options, assignment and sublease rights apply?

Option / Assign / Exit
04
Occupancy cost

The premises present well.

What will outgoings, services, fitout and relocation cost across the term, and which of them sit with the tenant?

Outgoings / Fitout / Total cost
05
Make-good

Make-good is standard.

What condition must the premises be returned in, what will that cost, and can the obligation be capped or scoped now?

Scope / Cap / Provision
06
Competition

There is another tenant interested.

What matters to the landlord besides face rent? Where is the tenant's walk-away point?

Terms / Leverage / Walk away

Common questions

Tenant representation in Brisbane

What is tenant representation?

A tenant representative acts only for the occupier — defining the requirement, searching on-market and off-market, shortlisting and inspecting, comparing options on total occupancy cost rather than headline rent, negotiating rent, incentive and term, and managing documentation through to occupation.

Is it worth it for a renewal?

Renewals are where the information gap is widest. The landlord knows what comparable space is leasing for and what incentives are being offered; most tenants do not. Testing the market before responding to a renewal offer is usually what changes the outcome — whether or not you end up moving.

Who pays the fee?

Fee structures vary by engagement and are agreed in writing before work begins. Some engagements are fee-for-service to the tenant; in others the fee is met from the leasing commission payable by the landlord. Either way the arrangement is disclosed at the outset.

How early should I start?

Earlier than most occupiers expect. Option dates and notice periods often fall well before expiry, and losing the option removes your main negotiating lever. Where a relocation or fitout is possible, the search, negotiation and works timeline needs to be planned around the expiry rather than compressed into its final months.
