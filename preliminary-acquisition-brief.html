What the brief covers

The requirement, the constraints and the risks.

Good acquisitions start with a clear brief. Without one, a search tends to produce listings that are judged against the vendor's marketing rather than the buyer's needs. Setting the criteria first means each opportunity can be tested against them.

Depending on the requirement, the brief may cover:

  • Intended use
  • Property type
  • Geographic area
  • Building and land area
  • Access and operational requirements
  • Budget
  • Finance and equity context, where relevant
  • Timing
  • Tenant and lease criteria for investments
  • Return and income considerations
  • Owner-occupier requirements
  • Key risks
  • Deal-breakers
  • Search flexibility

Not every item applies to every buyer. An owner-occupier brief usually centres on operational fit and capital commitment. An investment brief usually centres on the tenant, the lease and the income.

Common questions

About the Preliminary Acquisition Brief

What is a Preliminary Acquisition Brief?

It is a structured first step for a commercial property acquisition. Nick reviews what you are trying to acquire, identifies the constraints and unknowns that matter, and discusses with you whether the requirement is ready for a market search.

Is the Preliminary Acquisition Brief free?

Yes. The initial brief and the discussion with Nick are free, and there is no obligation.

Is this the same as appointing a buyer's agent?

No. Requesting a brief does not appoint Represent Commercial or commit you to anything. If you later want Represent Commercial to act for you through the search, assessment and negotiation, that is a separate Buyer's Agency and Acquisition Advisory engagement, subject to agreed fees.

Do I need to have a property in mind?

No. Many buyers start with a requirement rather than a property. If you are already considering one, say so in the form and the discussion can start there.

Can you help with owner-occupier acquisitions?

Yes. For a business buying its own premises, the brief centres on operational fit, the capital committed, the likely holding period and who would want the property later. If you are still deciding whether to buy at all, the lease vs buy guide sets out the trade-offs.

Can you help with commercial investment acquisitions?

Yes. For an investor, the brief centres on the income, the tenant and lease, what happens if the income stops, and the exit. How to avoid buying the wrong commercial property covers the main risks.

What areas do you cover?

Brisbane and South East Queensland. If your requirement is elsewhere in Queensland, include it in the form and Nick will tell you whether he can help.
