Commercial Property Leasing

Why is my commercial property not leasing?

Before reducing the rent or buying another advertising package, find where the campaign is losing suitable tenants. Price, competing stock, presentation, permitted use and the path from enquiry to inspection all need to be tested.

Nick O'TooleFounder & Director, Represent Commercial
Published 2 November 2026
Direct answer

Where should you start?

A commercial property can remain vacant because demand is weak, competing space offers better value, the rent or terms do not fit the market, the property does not suit the target occupier, or the campaign loses tenants through missing information and slow follow-up. Diagnose the cause from evidence before choosing the response.

Check demand and competing stock first

Define the occupier the property actually suits: use, floor area, access, power, clearance, parking, fitout and location. Check whether that occupier is active in the precinct and what alternatives they can inspect today. A general market headline does not tell you how your particular tenancy will perform.

Compare competing properties on usable space and total occupancy cost, including outgoings and incentives. Separate advertised asking rents from completed leasing evidence. Our precinct market pages provide context, but broad medians are not an appraisal of your individual property.

Test the rent and the terms

Ask the agent for the evidence behind the asking rent and how it compares with the best alternatives. A higher rent may be justified by better access, presentation or fitout, but the tenant needs to see that value. A price based only on the owner’s holding costs may not attract the market.

Look beyond face rent. Lease term, security, incentive, fitout responsibility and commencement timing can each change the decision. Use the Lease Incentive & Effective Rent Calculator to compare proposals on a consistent basis.

The Vacancy vs Rent Reduction Calculator tests the income cost of waiting against accepting a lower rent. Consider tenant quality, capital value and the calculator’s exclusions before setting the strategy.

Make it easy to assess the property

A tenant should be able to work out whether the space is worth inspecting without a series of unanswered questions. Check that the listing has current photography, a clear floor plan, area breakdown, asking terms, outgoings information, access, parking and relevant services.

State what is known about permitted use and approvals. Do not imply that every business can occupy the premises. Where the proposed use needs specialist confirmation, identify that early. Presentation work should address a specific leasing objection, with a cost and purpose agreed before money is spent.

Find where suitable tenants drop out

Review the campaign as a sequence: exposure, enquiry, qualification, inspection, offer and signed lease. Total enquiry is less useful than knowing which suitable occupiers progressed and why the others stopped.

  • Few enquiries: investigate demand, exposure, pricing and whether the listing communicates the offer.
  • Enquiries but few inspections: check suitability, unanswered questions, response time and access arrangements.
  • Inspections but no offers: collect specific feedback on condition, function, cost and competing options.
  • Offers that stall: identify the unresolved commercial term, approval or documentation issue.

These are diagnostic starting points, not proof of the cause. Ask for an enquiry and inspection log, follow-up outcomes and the next action on each live prospect.

Reset the campaign with a decision plan

Rank the barriers and agree what will change: positioning, property information, presentation, inspection access, asking terms or direct approaches to relevant occupiers. Set a review date and decide which evidence will show whether the change is helping.

More advertising may help when suitable tenants are not seeing the property. It will not resolve a use restriction or a cost position that those tenants cannot justify. Read our commercial leasing costs guide before committing to further spend.

Questions owners ask

Should I reduce the rent?

Where the asking position is above the evidence, a change may be needed. First compare the cost of waiting with the rent and terms a suitable tenant will accept. Where the barrier is access, missing information or property suitability, a rent reduction alone may not solve it.

Should I change leasing agents?

Consider a change when the current campaign has identifiable problems the agent is not addressing. Check the appointment and any continuing fee obligations before making a change. The underlying property and market still need an appropriate strategy.

What should I send for a leasing discussion?

Start with the property address, listing link, asking terms, vacancy period and recent enquiry or inspection feedback. Nick can then confirm the further information needed and whether a leasing appointment or Property Performance Review is appropriate.
