2027 CGT changes for commercial property owners: sell, hold or improve?

For owners who already hold industrial, office or retail property in Brisbane and South East Queensland. The tax rules are federal. The property decision depends on the asset in front of you.

Nick O'TooleFounder & Director, Represent Commercial. Guidance checked 28 November 2026
Direct answer

Should I sell my commercial property before 30 June 2027?

Not because of the date alone. 30 June 2027 is a transition reference point, not a blanket deadline to sell or to obtain a valuation.

For eligible assets held before 1 July 2027, the gain that accrued before that date and the gain that accrues after it are treated differently when the property is later sold (ATO, Capital gains tax reform).

Whether to sell, hold or improve depends on the asset, the market and your objectives. Your accountant should confirm how the rules apply to your ownership structure and circumstances.

What changes on 1 July 2027 for commercial property owners?

The 2027 CGT changes for commercial property come from the first stage of the Government's CGT reform. That stage is now law as the Treasury Laws Amendment (Tax Reform No. 1) Act 2026 (Act No. 49 of 2026).

The ATO says that from 1 July 2027 the changes will (ATO, Capital gains tax reform):

  • replace the 50% CGT discount for resident individuals, trusts and partnerships with cost base indexation, for gains that accrue on or after 1 July 2027;
  • introduce a minimum 30% tax for individuals on certain net capital gains that accrue on or after that date;
  • bring gains accruing from 1 July 2027 on pre-CGT assets (acquired before 20 September 1985) into CGT.

Cost base indexation adjusts the purchase price for inflation using the consumer price index before the gain is worked out.

If you already own the property

For an eligible asset bought before 1 July 2027 and sold on or after that date, the ATO describes two parts (ATO, Capital gains tax reform):

  • Gain before 1 July 2027. Current arrangements apply, including the 50% discount where eligible.
  • Gain from 1 July 2027. The new arrangements apply, using the asset's value at 1 July 2027 as its cost base.

The law treats the asset as if it were disposed of immediately before 1 July 2027 and reacquired on 1 July 2027. Any gain or loss from that deemed disposal is deferred. It is only reported when the property is later sold or another CGT event happens. Nothing is payable just because the date passes.

Why eligibility and ownership structure matter

The ATO describes these changes for individuals and trustees, and some exemptions will apply. How a gain is calculated depends on when the asset was acquired, how long it has been held and, for some assets, choices available under the law. Commercial property is often held through a trust, a company or a self-managed super fund. Your accountant should confirm which rules apply to your structure. This guide does not cover how each structure is treated.

What is enacted and what is still to come

  • Enacted. The first stage, passed by Parliament on 26 June 2026 (ATO, CGT reform: guidance and market valuations).
  • Still being developed. An alternative valuation method, the apportionment method. The ATO says it will provide further guidance once that method has been enacted.
  • Still to be published. ATO guidance on how to determine market value for certain assets, which professionals to engage and which records to keep. The ATO says further stages of legislation may follow.

Do I need to sell by 30 June 2027?

No date, on its own, tells you whether to sell. The transition changes how a future gain is split and taxed. It does not change the rent, the lease, the vacancy, the building or the buyer pool. Those factors still decide what the property is worth and what to do with it.

A sale rushed to meet a date can cost more than the tax difference it was meant to capture. For example, it could mean selling a partly vacant building before it is re-let, or selling just before a lease renewal would have settled the income.

CGT contract date or settlement date?

Where there is a contract of sale, the CGT event generally happens when you enter into the contract, not at settlement (ATO, CGT events). For a sale under contract, the contract date is generally the relevant date.

Timing for an individual sale, including conditional contracts, options and settlement terms, should be confirmed with your accountant and solicitor before you commit.

Do I need a CGT valuation before 1 July 2027?

Two dates are easy to confuse:

  • The date of the value. For most CGT assets, including real estate, you may need to establish the market value immediately before 1 July 2027 when you later sell or another CGT event happens (ATO, Capital gains tax reform).
  • The date a valuation report is prepared. The ATO says owners do not need a valuation in place on that date and should not obtain one before it, because a valuation cannot be forward-looking (ATO, CGT reform: guidance and market valuations).

The ATO also says that, for most individuals and trustees, the earliest return that will need to include a gain worked out this way is the 2027–28 return, and only if a CGT event happens in that year.

The ATO has said owners may be able to choose the apportionment method instead of a market valuation. That method is still being developed and is not yet available. Ask your accountant whether it may suit you once it is enacted.

What to gather now

Good records will support whichever method applies. The ATO notes that existing record-keeping requirements continue. Useful material includes:

  • Purchase contract, settlement statement and acquisition costs
  • Leases, variations, options and any side agreements
  • A current rent schedule and outgoings recoveries
  • Records of capital works and improvements, with dates and costs
  • Vacancy history and letting activity
  • Comparable lease and sale evidence around mid-2027

Represent Commercial does not provide formal valuations. Where a valuation is needed for tax purposes, it should come from a qualified valuer, engaged at the right time on your accountant's advice.

Sell, hold or improve: what should I check?

The same facts can point different ways depending on the owner's objectives, borrowings and time horizon. The table sets out what each fact can mean for each option.

Renewing a lease or spending on works does not always add value. A renewal at the wrong rent, or with heavy incentives, can reduce what the property is worth. Works that tenants in that precinct will not pay for are a cost, not an improvement.

Should I sell my warehouse vacant or leased?

Take an industrial unit in a southern Brisbane precinct such as Acacia Ridge, with the current lease expiring in 2027. The owner could sell it with vacant possession, or wait to sell until a new lease is in place.

  • Vacant possession may appeal to an owner-occupier who wants to operate from the building and will pay for control and timing.
  • A suitable lease may appeal to income-focused investors. That depends on the rent, the term and the tenant's covenant. A weak or below-market lease can work against the price.

Which works better depends on who is buying in that precinct at the time, and how long re-letting would take. Precinct evidence for Acacia Ridge, Brendale, Hemmant and Yatala is a useful starting point.
