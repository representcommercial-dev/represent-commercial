Renew or relocate

Renew or relocate? A commercial premises guide for Brisbane businesses.

A lease expiry is a decision point for the business, not only a rent negotiation. This guide sets out how I would compare staying with moving, for established businesses in Brisbane and South East Queensland.

Nick O'TooleFounder & Director, Represent Commercial. Published 16 November 2026
Direct answer

Should my business renew its lease or relocate?

There is no universal answer. Renewing avoids a new fit-out, downtime and the cost of moving, and the make-good on the current premises is deferred rather than triggered. Relocating can give the business premises that suit it better and, depending on the market, the premises and the landlord, a different rent or incentive.

Compare the two on the same basis: what the business needs from its premises, and the total cost of each option over the same term.

The question is not simply: “Is the new rent lower?”

It is: “Which premises serve the business best, at what total cost, over the next term?”

Start with the business requirement and timing

Before looking at rent, write down what the premises have to do for the next term: the space, layout, access, loading, parking, power and location the operation needs, and how the business expects to change. A renewal that locks in the wrong space can cost more than a higher rent in the right space.

Then set the timing. A relocation needs time to define the requirement, search, negotiate, document the lease and complete any fit-out before the current lease ends. A renewal negotiated with no credible alternative leaves the business with less to negotiate with.

Lease expiry, notice and option dates

Confirm the dates in the current lease first: the expiry, any option to renew and the window in which it must be exercised, notice periods, and any rent review that falls before expiry. These dates set the plan, and an option exercise window often closes well before the lease ends.

The lease sets these dates and how they operate. Ask your solicitor to confirm them and what exercising, or not exercising, an option commits the business to. I do not interpret the legal effect of a lease for clients.

Comparable premises and alternatives

The landlord’s renewal proposal is one option. Test it against what comparable premises are actually leasing for, including the incentives agreed, not only the advertised rents. Then identify premises that would genuinely suit the business if it moved.

Credible alternatives matter even if the business stays. They show what the market would offer, and they give the renewal negotiation a reference point. Represent Commercial publishes recorded lease and sale evidence for four industrial precincts on the precinct pages.

Effective occupancy cost, not headline rent

Headline rent is the least useful number in the comparison. Convert each option to its total cost over the same term: rent after incentives, outgoings the business pays, fit-out, relocation, overlap rent, and make-good on the premises being left.

The Lease Renewal vs Relocation Calculator puts staying and moving on one line, the Effective Rent Calculator converts an incentive package into one comparable rent, and the Occupancy Cost Comparison compares up to three premises on the same basis.

Incentives

Incentives are delivered as rent-free periods, rent abatements or fitout contributions. Each is worth a different amount depending on when it is received and what it can be spent on, so compare them as a value over the term rather than as a number of months.

Whether a renewing tenant or an incoming tenant is offered more depends on the market, the premises and the landlord. Do not assume that moving will bring a larger incentive, or that staying rules one out.

Fit-out, downtime and relocation

Moving has costs that do not appear in a rent comparison: the fit-out of the new premises, removals, IT and signage, any period of paying rent on both premises, and the time the business is disrupted. Some of these can be negotiated, such as a fitout contribution or an early access period for works.

Business disruption is real but hard to price. The renewal vs relocation calculator does not model it, so weigh it alongside the numbers rather than ignoring it.

Make-good

Make-good is the obligation to return the premises in the condition the lease requires at the end of the term. What it covers depends on how the lease is drafted, and the cost can be significant. Relocating usually triggers it; renewing usually defers it to a later expiry.

Have the obligation assessed early, so its cost is part of the comparison. When agreeing a new lease, the make-good scope is one of the terms worth negotiating before signing. The make-good negotiation guide covers records, scopes, costs and options in more detail.

How the comparison can play out

Illustrative example only

A hypothetical business occupies a 1,000m² warehouse and is comparing a five-year renewal with a move to similar premises nearby. The figures are illustrative inputs chosen for this example. They are not market evidence and not a recorded transaction.

Renewal rent
$180/m² net
Renewal incentive
3 months rent free
New premises rent
$165/m² net
New lease incentive
6 months rent free
Fit-out of new premises
$150,000
Removals and relocation
$60,000
Make-good on current premises
$80,000
Overlap rent
1 month at $180/m²

Over five years, the renewal costs $855,000 in rent after the rent-free period. The move costs $742,500 in rent after its longer rent-free period, plus $305,000 of fit-out, relocation, make-good and overlap rent: $1,047,500 in total. In this example, the lower rent and larger incentive do not cover the cost of moving.

Change the inputs and the answer can change. A larger fitout contribution, premises that suit the operation better, or a renewal that leaves the business in the wrong space can all reverse it. The example assumes flat rents with no annual rent reviews. Outgoings are assumed to be similar in both premises and are excluded, and the figures are undiscounted.

Practical next steps

Before a business renews or relocates, I would want these answered.

  1. What are the dates? Expiry, option and notice dates confirmed with your solicitor.
  2. What does the business need? A written requirement for the next term, not the last one.
  3. What would the market offer? Comparable rents, incentives and premises that would genuinely suit.
  4. What does each option cost in total? Rent after incentives, outgoings, fit-out, relocation and make-good over the same term.
  5. What is the walk-away position? The point at which the business would move rather than accept the renewal, or stay rather than move.
