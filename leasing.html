Where the time goes

Why commercial space stays vacant

Prolonged vacancy is rarely a market problem on its own. It is usually a stack of small frictions, each of which loses a tenant who was otherwise interested.

01

The campaign has gone stale

Same photos, same copy, same price for months. The market has already seen it and moved on, and nothing has changed to bring anyone back.

02

The asking rent has no evidence behind it

A number set from what the owner needs, or from what the property achieved three years ago, rather than from what comparable space has actually leased for.

03

Enquiries wait

A tenant enquiring on four properties signs with whoever engages first. A response measured in days rather than hours loses deals that were never reported as lost.

04

The listing answers nothing

No area breakdown, no outgoings, no clearance height, no access detail, no floor plan. The tenant cannot qualify the space, so they skip it.

05

Presentation undersells the space

Poor photography, a make-good not completed, services untested. Tenants discount what they cannot picture themselves operating in.

06

Inspections are hard to arrange

Keys held elsewhere, access by appointment only in business hours, days between request and viewing. Interest cools in the gap.

07

Enquiry does not convert to inspection

Volume looks reasonable but almost nobody views the property. That gap is a pricing, information or responsiveness problem, and it is measurable.

08

You are told nothing useful

No feedback on who enquired, what they asked, why they passed or what the market is telling you about the price. Without that, the owner cannot make a decision.

How we run a leasing campaign

REMOVE THE FRICTION. THEN THE MARKET can decide.

None of this is exotic. It is the ordinary work of leasing, done deliberately rather than left to whoever happens to pick up the phone.

— 01

Enquiries acknowledged quickly

The first response is the one that decides whether you are on the shortlist.

Enquiries come to the principal and are acknowledged promptly, with the information the tenant needs to qualify the space attached rather than promised.

— 02

Clear pricing and occupancy cost

A tenant comparing four options is comparing total cost, not headline rent.

Rent, outgoings, recoverable items and incentive set out plainly, so a prospective tenant can work out what the space actually costs them without a negotiation to find out.

— 03

Easier inspection pathways

Interest decays fast. The viewing should happen while it is still warm.

Access arranged without unnecessary steps, including outside standard hours where the tenant's operation requires it, and the principal attends rather than sending a key.

— 04

Property information prepared properly

Every unanswered question is a reason to look elsewhere.

Areas, services, clearance, access, parking, permitted use and a floor plan assembled before the property goes to market, not gathered in response to each enquiry.

Common questions

Commercial leasing in Brisbane

Why is my property still vacant?

Usually one of four things: the asking rent is above what recent evidence supports, the listing does not answer the questions a tenant needs answered, enquiries are not being responded to quickly enough, or inspections are too hard to arrange. Each is fixable — but each needs to be identified rather than assumed.

Do I have to drop the rent?

Not necessarily, but the asking rent needs a defensible basis. Where the price sits above the evidence, the incentive, fitout contribution or term usually has to carry the difference. That should be a deliberate decision with the net effective position understood, not something discovered after six months of no enquiry.

How long will it take to lease?

It depends on the precinct, the space, the price and how the property is presented, and no honest answer is a fixed number of weeks. What we can tell you at the outset is what comparable space has been leasing for, and how long comparable campaigns have taken in that precinct.

Can you lease a property you don't manage?

Yes. Leasing can be a standalone engagement. Where we also manage the property, leasing starts before the space is empty — renewal intent tested early and the rent position set ahead of the expiry rather than after it.
